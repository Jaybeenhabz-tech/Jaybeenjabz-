# Gender-based violence — global support and resources

This document provides respectful, survivor-centered guidance, safety steps, and links to trusted international resources. It is intended as a starting point — please add local hotlines and organizations for your country or region.

## If someone is in immediate danger
- Call your local emergency services right away (for example, 112 or 911 where applicable).
- If you are with the person, prioritize safety: remove them from immediate danger when safe to do so, or call emergency services for help.

## How to respond if someone discloses abuse
- Believe them and listen without judgment.
- Prioritize their safety and autonomy: ask what they need and what they want to do next.
- Respect their privacy and do not share identifying details without consent unless required by law or there is imminent danger.
- Offer to help connect them with professional services (medical, mental health, legal, shelter) or to accompany them when seeking help.

## Medical and forensic care
- If there was recent physical or sexual assault, encourage the survivor to seek urgent medical care — there may be medical needs and options to preserve evidence.
- Hospitals and sexual assault response teams can provide confidential medical care, STI prevention, emergency contraception (where applicable), and documentation.

## Reporting, legal options, and evidence preservation
- Reporting to police is a choice; support survivors in understanding options rather than pressuring them.
- If they wish to preserve evidence, advise them not to wash, change clothes, or clean the location before seeking medical/forensic help.
- Local NGOs and legal aid organizations often provide help understanding protections, restraining orders, and court processes.

## Safety planning and shelter
- Safety planning is personalized; support can include a packed-bag plan, emergency contacts, safe places to go, and secure storage of important documents.
- If immediate relocation is needed, shelters and crisis centers can provide temporary safe accommodation and practical support.

## Mental health and ongoing support
- Survivors often benefit from trauma-informed therapy, peer support groups, and counseling.
- Crisis hotlines can provide immediate emotional support and referrals.

## Support for bystanders and communities
- If you witness violence, call emergency services rather than intervening physically unless it is safe to do so.
- Offer support to survivors afterward: believe them, help them access services, and avoid victim-blaming language.
- Community education and bystander intervention training reduce harm at scale.

## Global and trusted resources (start here)
- World Health Organization (WHO) — Violence against women: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women
- UN Women — Ending violence against women: https://www.unwomen.org/en/what-we-do/ending-violence-against-women
- Helplinelists — international directory of domestic/sexual violence hotlines: https://www.helplinelists.org/
- RAINN (Rape, Abuse & Incest National Network) — resources for survivors (US-focused): https://www.rainn.org/
- National Domestic Violence Hotline (US) — https://www.thehotline.org/
- 1800RESPECT (Australia) — https://www.1800respect.org.au/
- Women's Aid (UK) — https://www.womensaid.org.uk/

Note: the lists above include global and country-specific pages; use the helplinelists directory or a web search like "domestic violence hotline <your country>" to find local numbers and services.

## Accessibility, language, and translation
- Provide information in the survivor's preferred language when possible; many hotlines offer multilingual support or can connect to translators.
- Use accessible formats (large text, screen-reader-friendly pages) and respect communication needs.

## Privacy, data, and online safety
- Be cautious when storing or sharing digital records of abuse (messages, photos). Use secure devices and private accounts, and get consent before sharing.
- If the abuser monitors devices, consider using a safer device or seeking assistance from a trusted organization.

## How to contribute and localize
- Please add country-specific emergency numbers, hotlines, and local services by opening a pull request.
- If you maintain local resources, consider adding a localized file (for example: GENDER-BASED-VIOLENCE--[COUNTRY].md) and link it from this page.

## Tone and survivor-centered language
- Use non-judgmental, empowering language: describe actions that respect survivors' choices and dignity.
- Avoid graphic detail and provide trigger warnings where deeper information is necessary.

## Further reading and organizations

- UN Women resources and research: https://www.unwomen.org/
- International and national NGOs often post up-to-date helpline and shelter lists; prefer official NGO or government sources for local contact details.
